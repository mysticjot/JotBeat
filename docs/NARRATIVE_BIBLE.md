# SALTBOUND: The Sunken Seal — Narrative Bible

> Canon as approved by the Creative Director (2026-08-16). Owned by the Narrative Designer; consumed by Artist and Sound. **Sections marked CANON are fixed — do not contradict them. Sections marked OPEN are undecided; proposals go to the BACKLOG Proposed section, never straight into canon.**

## World

The kingdom of **Asterfell** sank in a single night — an event remembered as **the Drowning**. (CANON)

Its vaults were sealed with **sigils**, and the keys to those vaults were scattered *deeper than the vaults themselves* — so that reaching a key is always harder than reaching the door it opens. (CANON)

The water is not empty. The Drowning was not a disaster; it was a **sealing**. Asterfell sank *something* by sinking itself. What lies below is not named in Game 1 — but it knocks. (CANON — saga spine)

**One-sentence fantasy:** you are a thief who dives into a drowned kingdom to steal the key to its deepest lock — and the air in your lungs is counting down.

## Protagonist

**Maren**, a **tide-thief** — one of the few who dive the sunken vaults for salvage and sigil-work. She dives for a patron known only as **the Curator**. (CANON)

Maren carries a **lungstone**: an enchanted air relic that sustains her underwater. The lungstone is the game's oxygen timer — the mechanic *is* the lore. When the water changes, the stone drains faster. This is always expressed as a tuning change (drain rate), never a new mechanic. (CANON)

Voice: Maren is a professional. She does not monologue; she assesses. OPEN: whether Maren ever speaks on-screen in Game 1 (default: she does not — the Victory card and the Curator carry the words).

## The Curator

Maren's patron. The **last seal-keeper** — heir to the sigil craft that sealed the vaults. (CANON — saga spine)

The Curator's full motive is not revealed in Game 1. What Game 1 establishes: the Curator knows more about what the doors are *for* than any employer should. (CANON)

## Game 1 arc — The Sunken Seal (CANON, beat by beat)

1. **Routine job.** A dive like any other: enter the vault, find the key, open the door, get out. The greybox pilot's loop is the diegetic loop — no contradiction between mechanics and story.
2. **Lift the Seal-Key.** The key Maren takes is not salvage. It is the **Seal-Key**.
3. **The water changes.** The lungstone drains faster. (Tuning change on the oxygen timer — no new mechanic.)
4. **The iron door.** Maren reaches and opens the heavy iron door — presented to the player as the escape.
5. **Victory card reveal.** *"The door you opened wasn't the exit. It was the lock."*
6. **Curator handoff.** *"Do you know what this opens?"*
7. **The knock from below.** Something answers.
8. **Hook card:** *SALTBOUND will return in **The Iron Chapel**.*

## Saga spine (CANON)

- The Drowning was Asterfell sealing something by sinking it.
- The Curator is the last seal-keeper.
- The saga ends with a **re-seal-or-open choice** — two endings.

Everything in every game must be writable without contradicting these three facts.

## Voice

How characters and UI text sound: spare, salt-rough, transactional. Nobody explains the world; the world is assumed. Cards and UI text are short declaratives, second person only on the reveal.

- Curator, at handoff: *"Do you know what this opens?"*

- Game Over (oxygen): OPEN — proposal: *"The lungstone is quiet."*

## Naming conventions

- **Places** are old-world and geological: Asterfell, the Iron Chapel. Vaults and doors are named for their seal, not their contents.
- **Relics** are compound nouns of body + stone or seal + key: lungstone, Seal-Key.
- **People** have short, working names: Maren. **Titles** replace names for the powerful: the Curator, the seal-keepers.
- The saga title is styled **SALTBOUND** (all caps on cards and title screen); game subtitles take "The + sealed thing": *The Sunken Seal*, *The Iron Chapel*.

## Mechanical canon (binding on design)

- The **oxygen timer is the lungstone**. UI, art, and audio treat it as a relic, not a bar.
- Faster drain after the Seal-Key is lifted is a **tuning change**, not a new mechanic (CANON).
- The iron door the player opens is diegetically **a lock being opened, not an exit** — level and door art may foreshadow this (sigil markings), UI must not say so before the reveal.
